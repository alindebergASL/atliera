# Direction A — Editorial Intelligence

## Design thesis

Direction A treats the Account Home as an authored intelligence brief. The interface does not announce machinery or expose a dashboard. It composes one intellectual answer for the reader, then keeps exact basis close to each consequential sentence.

The account name is compact folio metadata. The draft interpretation—“Exception handling is a plausible discovery focus.”—is the visual hero. A question rail exposes the reasoning structure without becoming navigation, chat, or a wizard.

## Composition

- asymmetric desktop grid: authored reading stage plus a narrow noninteractive question rail;
- editorial serif for synthesized meaning and system sans for facts/trust;
- oversized `01` folio mark signals authorship rather than sequence or confidence;
- exact change enters as a two-column essay annotation, not a card;
- recommendation resolves into one plum editorial plane;
- primary action sits with the intellectual hero so evidence review or the admitted plan remains visible at every required viewport;
- mobile removes the sidebar and turns the reading guide into compact orientation while retaining the authored sequence.

## Atliera-specific reusable behaviors

1. **Evidence stitch:** a two-node line glyph binds a statement to exact support; the open variant marks evidence plus interpretation assumptions.
2. **Question rail:** a static reading guide reveals the answer's logic without implying workflow.
3. **Editorial folio:** the interpretation receives a numbered authored stage while account identity remains metadata.
4. **Composed next-move plane:** one inverse field gives the recommendation unmistakable closure without a generic card grid.

## Interaction

- no-plan primary: `Review the basis` → exact meaningful-change evidence;
- admitted-plan primary: `View existing meeting plan` → admitted plan only;
- `Exact support` and `Evidence + assumptions` remain statement-local;
- desktop disclosure is a right-edge drawer;
- mobile disclosure recomposes as a bottom sheet;
- native modal semantics provide focus containment; Close and Escape restore focus to the invoking statement/action.

## Responsive behavior

At desktop, whitespace and asymmetry produce editorial pace. At tablet, the question rail becomes a compact orientation strip. At mobile/320px, the interpretation, trust cue, evidence action, and primary action stay in the first reading stage; the factual essay and next-move plane follow without horizontal overflow.

## Future compatibility

`future-workshop-compatibility.html` demonstrates a static editorial folio that could later support `Account | Workshop` and the accepted two-input future composer. It is outside the active prototype, contains no interactive controls, and authorizes no C3 behavior.

## Known compromises and production risks

- Georgia/system-serif metrics vary by platform; a production type decision would require its own authorization.
- The intentionally quiet evidence stitch needs future representative-user terminology validation.
- Long plan content remains dense inside a sheet, although all structured fields and close criterion are reachable.
- This is static synthetic-fixture code, not production data binding.
- Browser and agent review do not substitute for zero-training user testing.

## Advisory tradeoff

Direction A is the calmer, lower-risk production language. It favors editorial confidence, comprehension, and premium restraint. Its cost is that the identity is less immediately dramatic and less overtly proprietary than Direction B.
